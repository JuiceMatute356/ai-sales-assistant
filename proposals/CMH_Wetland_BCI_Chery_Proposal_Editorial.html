Why this vehicle

A security fleet lives
or dies on uptime.

The vehicles that let a security operation down are rarely the ones that crash. They are the ones that will not start on a cold morning, that sit in a workshop waiting for a part, or that quietly burn a fortune in fuel over a year of long shifts. None of that shows on the day you buy. All of it shows on the contract you could not cover and the margin you did not keep.

The Tiggo Cross 1.5 LiT CVT is chosen here for the unglamorous reasons that matter to a fleet: a claimed 7.3 litres per 100 km, an automatic gearbox that keeps officers fresh across a shift, enough ground clearance and presence for mixed-condition access roads, and a warranty that caps your downside for years, not months. It is a vehicle built to be driven hard and kept cheap.

Buy fifteen cars and you have an expense. Buy fifteen vehicles that start every morning, cost little to run and come back from a service fast, and you have a fleet that pays you back on every shift it covers.
